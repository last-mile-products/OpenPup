We are building a **real-life mini robot puppy**.

Not a toy you buy in a box and forget. A DIY one. We will sketch it, print parts, wire a Raspberry Pi, teach it to listen, and write down what happens — the victories and the “why is it spinning in a circle?” days.

The project is called **OpenPup** until the puppy earns a real name.

## Why a puppy?

A house robot should feel like a pet, not a vacuum. Puppies notice you. They follow. They make a little noise when they are happy. That is the vibe: a small wheeled (or later walking) friend that lives with us.

## The deal

- **Kids help design, name, test, and tell the story.** Ages ~8–12 means real jobs: photos, journal entries, picking colors, testing voice commands, holding parts while someone else screws them in.
- **Grown-ups handle power, soldering, and anything that can shock or pinch.**
- **Everything we learn goes on this website** so we can look back later and so other families can steal our notes.

## Two brains (on purpose)

The puppy will carry a **Raspberry Pi** with **OpenClaw** installed — that is the brain in its body. When a job is too hard for a little computer on batteries (long thinking, big memory, heavy tools), it will ask a **local server in the house**.

More on that in the next journal post, and on the [How it works](/how-it-works) page.

## What “done” looks like someday

1. It notices someone in the room.
2. It can talk and listen.
3. It can roll over and say hi.
4. Hard questions bounce to the house computer, then the puppy answers in its own voice.
5. This site is full of pictures, videos, and “remember when…” posts.

Today is Day 0. We have a folder, a name, and a plan. Tomorrow we start filling it with real mess.
